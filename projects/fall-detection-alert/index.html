Fall Detection Alert

Raises an alert about a second after a person ends up lying on the ground. YOLO pose keypoints and multi-person tracking feed a small state machine per person, driven by rules you can read: torso angle, body shape and hip speed. No training data, no GPU: it runs on a laptop CPU and explains every alert it raises.

How it works

01Pose keypoints in, explainable alerts out

Each frame goes through a pose model with a tracker, so every person keeps an ID. From the shoulders and hips the pipeline computes three simple features, and a state machine per person turns them into an alert only after a lying posture is held, which filters out bending, sitting and quick crouches.

The state machine is what keeps false alarms down: a fall must be followed by a lying posture held for one second before the alert fires, and the alert closes on its own when the person stands up again.

Torso angle θ
Angle of the shoulder-to-hip line from vertical: about 0° standing, about 90° lying.
Aspect ratio w/h
Width over height of the person box; the fallback when shoulders or hips are hidden.
Hip velocity v
Downward speed of the hips in body heights per second, so it does not depend on distance to the camera.

Design decisions

04Why it is built this way

Rules instead of a trained classifier

Every threshold has a physical meaning and can be tuned from the CLI, and every alert can be explained from the trace. No fall dataset is needed to start.

Confirmation time

An alert needs a lying posture held for 1.0 s, with short gaps tolerated. That removes alarms from bending, tying shoes or sitting down quickly.

Velocity in body heights

Normalizing the hip speed by the person's height makes the same threshold work near and far from the camera.

Graceful degradation

When shoulders or hips are not visible, the box aspect ratio takes over, so occlusion does not silence the detector.

Objects are not people

A track can only fall after it showed a body pose (6 confident keypoints) in some frame. Bins, coats and shadows detected as a person never do, so their wide boxes no longer raise alerts.

Repairing id switches

When a person falls the box goes from tall to wide and the tracker often starts a new id. A new track that appears within one body height of a track lost moments ago continues its state and alert.
